Camping in Colorado State Parks

Colorado's state parks offer some of the most comfortable camping in the state, with reservable sites, many with electric hookups and showers, often beside a reservoir. They also come with their own pass, booking system and rules.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife manages the state's park system, which spans plains reservoirs, foothill forests, red-rock canyons and high mountain lakes. For many families, state parks are the easiest way into Colorado camping: sites are reservable, roads are paved or well graded, restrooms are close and rangers are on duty. The trade-off is cost and crowds, especially on summer weekends near the Front Range.

What you get at a state park campground

Amenities vary by park and loop, but CPW campgrounds commonly offer:

  • Site typesBasic tent sites, electric sites and, at some parks, full hookups for RVs.
  • FacilitiesRestrooms in most loops, with showers and laundry at many larger parks.
  • LodgingCabins and yurts at a number of parks, bookable year-round at some locations.
  • RecreationBoating, fishing, swim beaches, trails and ranger programs depending on the park.

Site counts and exact amenities are listed on each park's "Camping & Lodging" page on the CPW website. Because they change, we link to the official pages rather than repeat numbers.

Passes: the part that confuses everyone

A campsite reservation is not the same as park entry. CPW requires a valid parks pass on each vehicle in addition to your camping fee. There are several ways to meet that requirement:

Daily vehicle pass

Bought at the park entrance station or self-service kiosk, valid for the day. Some parks charge more than others, and out-of-state vehicles may pay a higher rate at some parks. If you are camping several nights, check whether you need a pass for each day.

Keep Colorado Wild Pass (residents)

Colorado residents can add the Keep Colorado Wild Pass when they register a car, motorcycle, recreational vehicle or light truck through the DMV. It is linked to that license plate, valid for a year or until the registration expires, and shown at parks with your registration card or digitally on your phone. It can only be purchased through vehicle registration, not at CPW locations, and it does not cover federal fee areas, state wildlife areas or state trust lands. Residents who opt out at registration can buy other annual passes from CPW.

Other annual passes

CPW also sells annual passes for vehicles not covered by Keep Colorado Wild, including options for nonresidents, and discounted passes for qualifying residents. Eligible military and disabled veteran plate holders may receive the Keep Colorado Wild Pass at no cost. Current pass types and prices are on the CPW parks passes page.

Federal passes do not work here

An America the Beautiful pass covers national parks and many federal fee areas, but not Colorado state parks. Likewise, a state parks pass does not cover national park entrance fees.

Booking a state park site

  1. Find the park and loop on the CPW reservation site, using the map view to compare sites by size, hookups and shade.
  2. Book up to six months ahead. CPW opens dates on a rolling six-month window, released at midnight Mountain Time.
  3. Check vehicle limits for your RV or trailer length and the number of vehicles allowed per site.
  4. Read the park alerts for closures, water restrictions, fire bans or construction before you go.
  5. Arrive within the window. If you will be more than a day late, contact the park; otherwise your site may be resold.

For more on timing, cancellations and backup strategies, see our reservations guide.

Key rules in state park campgrounds

  • Stay limit: CPW lists a maximum stay of 14 days per park in any 28-day period.
  • Check-in and check-out: Campsites commonly use a 1 p.m. check-in and noon check-out; cabins and yurts often check in later in the afternoon.
  • Pets: Statewide rules require pets to be under control and on a leash of six feet or less, unless in a vehicle or a designated off-leash area. Some parks restrict pets further, so check before booking.
  • Quiet hours and occupancy: Each campground posts quiet hours and limits on people, tents and vehicles per site.
  • Fires: Use only provided fire rings or grills, and follow any fire restrictions in effect. Buy firewood locally.
  • Wildlife: Store food, coolers and scented items in a hard-sided vehicle or provided locker. CPW's campsite guidance covers this in detail; see our bear safety guide.

Cabins and yurts

CPW rents cabins and yurts at a number of state parks, which makes shoulder-season and winter camping far more comfortable. Some are rustic with bunks and a heat source; others are more fully equipped. Bring your own bedding unless the listing says otherwise, and check the specific check-in time, which is often later than for campsites so crews can clean between guests. Popular cabins book out quickly for fall weekends.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a parks pass if I have a campsite reservation in a Colorado state park?

Yes. CPW requires a valid state parks pass for each vehicle in addition to the camping reservation. That can be a daily pass bought at the park or an annual pass such as the Keep Colorado Wild Pass.

What is the Keep Colorado Wild Pass?

It is an annual state parks pass that Colorado residents can buy when they register a car, motorcycle, recreational vehicle or light truck through the DMV. It is linked to the license plate and is valid for a year or until the registration expires. It cannot be bought at CPW locations, and it does not cover federal fee areas, state wildlife areas or state trust lands.

Are dogs allowed in Colorado state park campgrounds?

Generally yes, if they are under control and on a leash no longer than six feet, according to CPW regulations. Some parks are stricter; CPW notes, for example, that Roxborough State Park does not allow dogs, and Mueller State Park limits where dogs can go.

What time is check-in at Colorado state park campgrounds?

CPW's park pages commonly list a 1 p.m. check-in and noon check-out for campsites. Cabins and yurts often have later check-in times. Confirm on your reservation.
