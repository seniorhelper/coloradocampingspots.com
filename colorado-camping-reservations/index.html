How Colorado Campsite Reservations Work

Colorado campsites are booked through two main systems that run on different rules: Recreation.gov for federal land and Colorado Parks and Wildlife's own system for state parks. Knowing which one you need, and when sites release, is most of the battle.

Updated October 2026 · About 6 min read · By the Colorado Camping Spots editors

The single most common reason a Colorado camping trip falls apart is the reservation, not the weather. Popular campgrounds near Denver, in Rocky Mountain National Park and around Aspen can be fully booked for summer weekends within minutes of opening. The good news is that the systems are predictable once you understand them, and cancellations constantly return sites to the pool.

Recreation.gov: how federal sites open

Recreation.gov is the federal booking platform for campgrounds, permits and timed entry across agencies. Each campground sets its own booking window, and that window is listed on the campground's page.

  • Six-month rollingThe most common pattern for national forest campgrounds. For example, the White River National Forest says most of its developed campgrounds take reservations up to six months ahead.
  • Shorter windowsSome campgrounds hold sites for short-notice bookings. Routt County forest campgrounds have tested a two-week window, and Saddlehorn at Colorado National Monument mixes six-month, two-week and same-day inventory.
  • Split inventoryThe BLM's Rabbit Valley sites have offered about half the sites on a six-month window and the rest on a four-day window.
  • Different parks, different rulesPinon Flats at Great Sand Dunes takes reservations up to three months ahead, with no first-come sites.

Release timing

Rolling windows release one new day of availability at a time. If a campground opens six months ahead, the dates six months out appear each day at the published release time. Check the campground listing for its release time, and note the time zone it is stated in, so you are not an hour or two late.

Rocky Mountain National Park

The park's Aspenglen, Glacier Basin, Moraine Park and Timber Creek campgrounds require reservations in summer and sell out in advance, and the park says there are no same-day sales at its campgrounds. Longs Peak Campground is the exception, a tent-only, first-come site. See our Rocky Mountain National Park camping guide for details, including how campground reservations interact with timed entry.

Colorado state parks: the CPW system

Colorado Parks and Wildlife runs its own reservation system for state park campsites, cabins and yurts. You can book online any time at cpwshop.com or by phone at 1-800-244-5613.

  1. Know the window. CPW says state park campsites can be reserved up to six months before arrival, and reservations go live at midnight Mountain Time for the date six months out.
  2. Plan on reserving. CPW's policies say campers who occupy a site without a reservation can be cited or evicted, so do not count on walking in.
  3. Get a parks pass. Every vehicle needs a valid state parks pass in addition to the campsite reservation. Colorado residents may already have the Keep Colorado Wild Pass through vehicle registration. See our state park camping guide.
  4. Arrive on time or call. CPW warns that if you do not show up within 24 hours of your check-in time and have not told the park you will be late, your site may be resold.
  5. Respect stay limits. CPW lists a 14-day maximum stay per park in any 28-day period.

Tactics that actually work

Book the exact moment the window opens

For summer holiday weekends at popular sites, set an alarm for the release time, sign in beforehand, and have two or three backup sites ready. Choose your dates and loop in advance so you can click through quickly.

Hunt for cancellations

Plans change constantly, and cancelled sites return to inventory. Checking a few days before your dates, and again the day before, often turns up a site in a fully booked campground. Weeknights are far easier than weekends.

Be flexible on place

A lesser-known campground an hour from the famous one is often wide open. Our best camping spots by region page can help you find alternatives. National forest campgrounds frequently have more availability than state or national parks in the same area.

Consider first-come sites and dispersed camping

Some national forest and BLM campgrounds remain first-come, first-served, and much of Colorado's national forest and BLM land allows free dispersed camping without any reservation. Arrive early in the day, especially on Fridays. Read our dispersed camping guide first, because the rules are specific.

Changes, cancellations and fees

Both systems charge reservation and change fees and set cancellation rules that depend on how close to arrival you cancel. These fees are revised periodically, so read the policy on the campground listing or CPW's reservation policy page before booking. If a fire closure, flood or other emergency closes a campground, the agency typically cancels affected reservations and explains refunds directly.

Before you click book

Check the campground page for vehicle length limits, tent pad sizes, whether water is on, and the opening and closing dates. High-elevation campgrounds often open late in spring if snow lingers, and water systems may not be turned on at the start of the season.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance can you reserve a campsite in Colorado?

Many sites open six months ahead on a rolling basis. Colorado Parks and Wildlife says state park campsites can be reserved up to six months before arrival. Many national forest campgrounds on Recreation.gov also use a six-month window, but some use shorter windows, such as two weeks, and Pinon Flats at Great Sand Dunes opens three months ahead. Check each campground's page.

Do Colorado state parks take walk-up campers?

Plan on a reservation. CPW moved its campgrounds to a reservation system, and its policies warn that campers who occupy a site without a reservation can be cited or evicted. Same-day bookings may be possible online when sites are open.

Are Colorado state parks on Recreation.gov?

No. State park campsites, cabins and yurts are reserved through Colorado Parks and Wildlife at cpwshop.com or by phone at 1-800-244-5613. Recreation.gov handles federal sites such as national parks and national forests.

What happens if I do not show up for my reservation?

Rules vary by system. CPW says that if you do not arrive within 24 hours of your scheduled check-in and do not tell the park you will be late, your site may be resold. Recreation.gov campgrounds publish their own no-show and cancellation rules on each listing.
