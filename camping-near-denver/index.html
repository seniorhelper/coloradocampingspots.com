Camping Near Denver

Some of Colorado's most convenient camping sits within an hour or two of downtown Denver, from reservoir campgrounds with full hookups to backcountry shelters in the foothills and designated free sites in the Pike National Forest.

Denver sits at the edge of the Front Range, so you can leave work on a Friday and be in a campsite before dark. The catch is that hundreds of thousands of other people can too. The closest and most popular sites book early for summer weekends, and the free dispersed areas near the city have strict rules after years of heavy use. Here is how the main options compare, roughly in order of distance from downtown.

Within the metro area

Cherry Creek State Park

A large reservoir park in Aurora, a short drive from downtown. CPW says basic tent and full-hookup campsites are available year-round, with amenities including showers, laundry, a dump station and firewood for sale. It is a practical choice for travelers with RVs, for a first night after arriving at Denver International Airport, or for a family overnight with the comforts of home close by. Expect city sounds and a busy summer beach.

Chatfield State Park

On the southwest edge of the metro area near Littleton, Chatfield wraps around a reservoir with mountain views. CPW lists four camping areas, many sites with full hookups that are seasonal, and electric-only sites available year-round; in winter only one loop stays open. The park is popular for boating, swimming and a large off-leash dog area, and it is close to Waterton Canyon and the start of the Colorado Trail.

Pass plus campsite

Both parks require a valid state parks pass on each vehicle in addition to your campsite reservation. See our state park camping guide for pass options.

In the foothills, under an hour

Golden Gate Canyon State Park

About a half hour past Golden, this forested park of aspen, pine and meadows feels like the mountains. CPW describes several ways to stay:

  • Reverend's RidgeThe family campground, with electric and tent sites; it stays open at limited capacity in winter.
  • Aspen MeadowA tents-only campground that typically closes from late September to mid-May.
  • BackcountryFour three-sided backcountry shelters sleeping up to six and twenty backcountry tent sites, each a 1.5 to 2 mile hike in, reservation required, no fires.
  • Cabins and yurtsAvailable by reservation year-round.

The park has no dispersed camping and no cell or internet service, so download your reservation and maps before you go.

Staunton State Park

West of Conifer, Staunton is one of Colorado's newer state parks, with granite cliffs, a waterfall trail and rock climbing. CPW lists campsites including hike-in tent sites added after a land acquisition allowed the trail network to be rerouted. Check the CPW page for current site types and how far you will carry your gear.

Pike National Forest, about an hour southwest

The South Platte Ranger District of the Pike National Forest covers the hills around Deckers, Buffalo Creek and Bailey. It has developed national forest campgrounds and is the main area for free camping close to Denver, under specific rules:

  1. Designated sites only. Dispersed camping is allowed only at designated sites marked with a "P" for parking and a tent symbol. No camping or parking outside them.
  2. Know the areas. The district names Buffalo Creek, Cedar Mountain, Geneva Creek, Hall Valley, Matukat and the Rampart Range Recreation Area as its primary dispersed areas.
  3. Some need reservations. Certain Rampart Range sites are booked through Recreation.gov.
  4. Know the closures. No dispersed camping along the South Platte River corridor from Cheesman Canyon to Foxton Road or along the Guanella Pass Scenic Byway. The Rampart Range road closes to passenger vehicles from December 1 to April 1.
  5. Bring everything. The district notes there are no hosts, trash service or water at dispersed sites.

Our dispersed camping guide explains stay limits, distances and Motor Vehicle Use Maps.

Higher and cooler: Indian Peaks and Brainard Lake

West of Boulder near Ward and Nederland, the Brainard Lake Recreation Area sits at the edge of the Indian Peaks Wilderness, with lakes below the Continental Divide. It has used timed vehicle tickets on Recreation.gov, including an overnight option, and its campground is reserved separately. Nights here are cold even in midsummer, and the season is short. Backpacking into the Indian Peaks requires a permit from June 1 to September 15; see our permits guide.

Farther afield, under two hours

Within roughly two hours you can also reach Mueller State Park near Divide, the Kenosha Pass area, national forest campgrounds along Guanella Pass, the Peak to Peak Highway campgrounds near Allenspark and Rocky Mountain National Park. Our best camping spots by region page and the full list of 100 spots cover these in detail, and our Rocky Mountain National Park camping guide explains its reservation and timed entry rules.

Tips for weekend campers from Denver

  • Book early or go midweek. Friday and Saturday nights from June to September are the hardest to get.
  • Have a backup. If you are counting on a first-come or dispersed site, choose two alternatives in the same area.
  • Leave early on Fridays. Traffic on US-285, I-70 and US-36 builds through the afternoon.
  • Check fire restrictions. Foothill counties and the Pike National Forest often restrict fires in dry spells.
  • Plan for bears. Even close to the city, bears raid campsites. Lock food in your vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the closest campground to downtown Denver?

Cherry Creek State Park in Aurora and Chatfield State Park near Littleton are both inside the Denver metro area. Cherry Creek offers basic tent and full-hookup sites year-round, and Chatfield offers full-hookup and electric sites, with electric-only sites available year-round.

Is there free camping near Denver?

Yes, in the national forests. Southwest of Denver, the Pike National Forest's South Platte Ranger District allows dispersed camping only at designated, signed sites in areas including Buffalo Creek, Cedar Mountain, Geneva Creek, Hall Valley, Matukat and the Rampart Range Recreation Area. Some Rampart Range sites require reservations.

Can you camp at Golden Gate Canyon State Park without a reservation?

Plan on a reservation. Golden Gate Canyon's campgrounds, cabins, yurts and backcountry sites are reserved through CPW, and the park says backcountry camping requires a reservation. The park has no dispersed camping and no cell service.

When should I book camping near Denver?

As early as the booking window allows for summer weekends, which is up to six months ahead for state parks. Weeknights, spring and fall are much easier, and cancellations open up regularly in the final days before a trip.
